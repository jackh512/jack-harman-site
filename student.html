Why this path

An entrepreneur who knows the numbers.

A lot of business owners fail not because their idea is bad, but because they don't understand their own numbers. I learned that firsthand. On paper, Junior Distribution looked great, and our sales numbers were strong. But the margins were too thin to hire a single employee, and I had to shut it down.

That's exactly why I paired an Entrepreneurship major with an Accounting minor. Entrepreneurship teaches me how to find opportunities, build products, and sell them. Accounting teaches me how to price them, track costs, and know whether a business is truly profitable before it grows.

My goal is to be a business owner who understands the numbers inside and out, so I never make the same mistake twice.

Coursework

Classes that shaped me

MGT 354

Growing the New Venture

This class focused on what happens after launch: scaling operations, hiring, and funding growth, which are the exact challenges that stopped Junior Distribution and that I'm now planning for at Artemin AI.

MKT 340

Professional Selling

Selling has been at the center of everything I've done, from schools and local businesses at Custom Hub to B2B relationships at Harman Corp, and this class gave me a real process for building trust and closing deals.

MKT 385

Marketing for Entrepreneurs

My very first business failed because I didn't know how to reach customers, so learning to build a brand and market it on a startup budget, starting with my own personal brand on this site, fills that exact gap.

ACC 212

Managerial Accounting

Managerial accounting taught me to break down costs, margins, and break-even points, the numbers I wish I had mastered before Junior Distribution, and the ones that matter most for helping manufacturers cut costs at Artemin AI.
